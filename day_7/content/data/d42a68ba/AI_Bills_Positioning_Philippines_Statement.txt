### Statement on the Positioning of AI Bills in the Philippines: Impact of Penalties and the Feasibility of AI Application Directories

#### Context and Overview

The Philippines is currently navigating a pivotal stage in its efforts to establish a robust framework for artificial intelligence (AI) governance, influenced by a range of proposed legislative measures aimed at regulating AI technologies. These initiatives, now pending in Congress, address a spectrum of AI-related concerns, particularly emphasizing issues of unregulated systems, misuse of AI technology, and the ethical implications of AI deployment in various sectors.

#### Impacts of Proposed Penalties

The proposed legislation, particularly the Artificial Intelligence Regulation Act (House Bill 7913), introduces significant penalties for violations of AI regulations, which include the development and deployment of unregistered AI systems. Offenders may face severe financial penalties ranging from PHP 500,000 to PHP 10,000,000, along with potential imprisonment for up to 12 years. Such stringent measures are aimed at deterring unethical AI practices, including disinformation and privacy breaches. However, these extensive penalties may create a chilling effect on innovation, particularly for startups and smaller companies that may lack the resources to navigate complex compliance requirements effectively.

#### Directory of AI Applications: Feasibility and Implications

The proposal for a comprehensive directory of all AI applications in the Philippines presents both opportunities and challenges. On one hand, such a centralized directory could serve as a vital resource for fostering collaboration among researchers, practitioners, and policymakers, enhancing transparency, guiding ethical AI practices, and promoting investment in AI development.

Conversely, the operational challenges of maintaining a continually updated, exhaustive registry could impede innovation in a fast-evolving field like AI. The administrative burden may slow down the agility required for timely advancements, and excessive regulation could deter new entrants into the market, ultimately stifling job creation and technological progress.

#### Global Perspective on AI Legislation

A review of global trends reveals varying methodologies in the approach to AI legislation. For example, the European Union has introduced comprehensive regulations that emphasize ethical use and accountability, promoting a collaborative environment for AI development while safeguarding citizens' rights. In contrast, the United States appears to adopt a more decentralized approach, prioritizing innovation and flexibility without stringent regulatory frameworks.

Singapore adopts a model that strikes a balance between regulation and innovation support, encouraging responsible AI use through industry partnerships. These international examples could provide valuable insights for the Philippine government in crafting a balanced approach to AI governance that fosters innovation while protecting societal interests.

#### Conclusion

The regulatory landscape for AI in the Philippines is on the verge of significant transformation through the proposed AI bills. While the intention behind imposing penalties is to foster responsible practices, it is crucial to strike a balance that does not hinder innovation. Additionally, the viability of a centralized AI directory must be assessed in terms of its operational impacts on the industry, focusing on facilitating rather than obstructing progress. Policymakers should engage with stakeholders across various sectors to create an adaptable framework that supports AI's transformative potential while upholding ethical standards.
